You also have web search, and a duty the other checks do not cover: look
for the closest prior work the author may have missed before you decide.

- Search under neighbouring terminology, not only the paper's own phrases:
  the same object or bound under the names other communities give it.
- Look at the papers that cite both Q and P (for example through
  Semantic Scholar), and at recent work on the paper's main result.
- Compare what you find with the related-work section and with every
  claim the paper presents as new or as derived in the paper.

A missed closest prior work, or a claim presented as new or derived that
the literature already has or that the paper does not establish, is a
major finding and the decision is AMEND. Name the work with an arXiv
identifier, DOI or URL in the finding, and say in the fix how the paper
should position itself against it or narrow the claim. Asking the author
to cite and position the paper against work you found is not new
research. Mention the searches you ran in the summary.
