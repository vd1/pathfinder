\section*{A sharp obstruction and a repaired target}

Let the risk-adjusted satisfaction be
\[
R_n(x_n)=-\operatorname{CVaR}_{\beta_n}(-\psi_n(x_n,\xi_n)).
\]
Translation equivariance gives
\[
-\operatorname{CVaR}_{\beta_n}(-[\psi_n(x_n,\xi_n)-t_n(s)])
=R_n(x_n)-t_n(s)
\]
because the payment is deterministic conditional on the message profile. Thus risk
adjustment does not itself alter the payment term.

Q's assumptions on the expected valuation do not imply its curvature assumptions
for \(R_n\). On \(\mathcal X=[-1,1]\), let two equiprobable satisfaction samples be
\[
\psi(x,1)=-3,\qquad \psi(x,2)=3-2x^2.
\]
Then
\[
V(x)=\mathbb E[\psi(x,\xi)]=-x^2,
\]
so \(V(0)=0\) and \(V\) is strongly concave. At tail mass
\(\beta=1/2\), however, the lower-tail satisfaction is
\[
R(x)=-\operatorname{CVaR}_{1/2}(-\psi(x,\xi))=-3,
\]
which is constant. After the harmless normalization \(R(x)-R(0)\), it is zero.
Hence Q's LMI uniqueness argument, which inserts the strong-concavity modulus into
the pseudogradient Jacobian, cannot be inferred after the CVaR substitution.

Conditional on separately assuming that every \(R_n\) has the required concavity
and regularity, Q's exact KKT matching extends using concave subgradients, and its
equilibrium payment algebra remains available. The learning problem is different.
P's fixed-radius, finite-batch oracle targets an expected empirical-smoothed
surrogate and leaves a true-objective gap of order
\[
D=O\left(\delta_{\max}+\frac{e_s}{\delta_{\min}}\right).
\]
It is not a proximal best-response oracle. A defensible joint target is therefore
a robust implementation theorem for a CVaR game learned with biased bandit
feedback. If true risk-adjusted welfare is \(m\)-strongly concave and a learned
allocation has welfare loss at most \(D\), then
\[
\|\widehat x-x^\star\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{2D}{m}}.
\]
On Q's capped message set from the learning section, Lipschitz payment and utility
maps then turn a full-strategy displacement into explicit budget-residual and
participation-slack bounds. This requires the price cap: Q's base mechanism defines
prices on the unbounded orthant, so global payment Lipschitzness is unavailable there.
An allocation-only bound does not control the price-dependent payments. Exact balance
and rationality hold at the exact equilibrium of a surrogate
game only if the mechanism actually implements that surrogate; they do not follow
for arbitrary off-equilibrium iterates of P's update. Controlling prices and proving
convergence of a game map, rather than a common-objective consensus map, are the
remaining technical tasks.
