# Assessment of Q and P

Q is *Competitive Market Behavior of LLMs*, a continuous double auction study. P is *When a Donor Wrist Cannot Locate a Rotating Held Object*, a robotics synthesis. P's internal labels "paper Q" and "paper P" refer to Aero Hand Open and DynaMAC, respectively, not to the assigned Q and P. Those robotics results cannot be attributed to the assigned Q.

The narrow connection is an information test. P's Section 3 defines object pose by \(T_o=T_wG\) and proves a conditional matched-observation limit: if two hidden orientations produce the same available observations but require different approaches, an observation-only receiver cannot guarantee the right approach in both. Q's Sections 3.3 and 4 give each trader its own reservation value, the standing quotes, and public and own histories, while evaluating realized surplus against the full-information maximum. The analogous question is whether two hidden market states can share one trader's information and require different bids for continuation welfare.

One conditional witness fits Q's mechanism. Take a late-round buyer with value \(v_B=3\), an outstanding ask of \(2.50\) from seller \(S_1\) with cost \(2.50\), and a standing bid strictly below \(2\). Suppose the buyer acts now, seller \(S_2\) is selected next, and then the iteration cap ends the round. In state A, hidden sellers \((S_2,S_3)\) have costs \((1,3)\); in state B, they have costs \((3,1)\). Seller \(S_3\) is not selected again. The buyer's visible history can be identical in both states. If the buyer posts an improving bid of \(2\), and \(S_2\) crosses whenever profitable, state A trades with \(S_2\), yielding continuation surplus \(3-1=2\). In state B, \(S_2\) cannot profitably cross or improve the \(2.50\) ask, so this action yields zero. If the buyer crosses the standing ask at \(2.50\), the continuation surplus is \(3-2.50=0.50\) in either state. Thus the statewise welfare-best immediate actions differ. Randomizing over the same observation cannot guarantee the best continuation in both. This is a conditional existence example, not a measurement of Q's markets.

The cost swap preserves Q's fixed seller-cost multiset without an earlier trade. In round one, a common history in which neither seller has made a revealing quote is feasible because Q selects each iteration from all agents who have not transacted, including agents selected before. The example conditions on the same late-round selection path and on \(S_2\)'s crossing policy. With an illustrative equal prior on the two states, the best information-limited action yields expected continuation surplus \(1\), while a state-informed action yields \(1.25\), a conditional gap of \(0.25\). This is a welfare oracle comparison; Q's buyer neither knows the next random selection nor necessarily maximizes social welfare. The example cannot explain Q's observed losses without estimating the frequency and posterior weight of such matched states. Near the iteration cap, a history with two uninformative sellers may itself be rare.

A useful experiment would replay matched public histories while swapping privately assigned values within Q's fixed schedule, hold the random-selection path and other agents' policies fixed, and compare an information-limited policy with a hidden-state oracle. The measured gap could separate losses intrinsic to the information interface from losses due to LLM decisions. It should also be compared with simple constrained zero-intelligence and human baselines. Q reports slow spread crossing and lower efficiency but no such decomposition (Sections 4.1 and 4.2). P offers the conditional logic but no auction experiment.

I do not see a publication result in the assigned pair alone. The existence proof is elementary, and information treatments and efficiency benchmarks in continuous double auctions have prior work: [Anufriev et al. (2022)](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/2xr9q-wnz20), [Anufriev et al., efficiency under evolutionary learning](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00191-011-0230-8), and [Gode and Sunder (1993)](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/pdf/10.1086/261868). A publishable contribution would need a measured conditional gap in Q's exact setting, a comparison with these baselines, and an observed effect on LLM inefficiency. Neither paper supplies those data.
