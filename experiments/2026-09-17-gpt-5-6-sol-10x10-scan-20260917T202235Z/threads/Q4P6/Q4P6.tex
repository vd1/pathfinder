\section{The pair and the connexion}

\emph{Mechanism Design for Alignment and Control} (Q) studies AI agents with unknown preferences and capabilities.  It seeks both honest revelation and obedient action under one-sided imitation, where capability can be concealed but not counterfeited, and characterizes implementability through nested cyclical monotonicity.  \emph{MARS-RA} (P) uses large-multimodal-model-generated pairwise contribution comparisons, rank aggregation, and potential-based reward shaping for cooperative multi-agent credit assignment.  The connexion pursued is to treat P's comparison-derived score as a reward-shaping instrument and ask whether the resulting reward policy remains implementable when a capable agent can suppress visible contribution \ledger{1, 15}.

\section{Supported findings}

The supported conclusion is a distinction, not a theorem about MARS-RA.  P claims robustness to noisy comparisons and changing participation, whereas Q addresses strategic hidden types and incentives.  Nothing in P's abstract says that agents report comparisons, or establishes robustness when they strategically alter evaluator-visible actions or information.  Thus P's advertised statistical robustness does not by itself imply Q-style incentive implementability; it is not evidence that MARS-RA actually violates implementability either \ledger{8, 15}.

This distinction motivates a precise research target: a two-agent, two-capability-type, two-action model with one-sided imitation and an external comparison oracle.  One would seek a comparison-score rule stable to noise or participation changes whose induced reward policy violates nested cyclical monotonicity because a high-capability agent can imitate a low type by sandbagging.  A monotone reward coupling, audit, or peer-scoring layer could then be studied as a repair.  The counterexample and repair are proposals, not established findings \ledger{16, 21}.

\section{Objections and unresolved issues}

The evidence is thin: the supplied inputs contain only titles and abstracts, and the peer directories contain no additional notes.  In particular, they do not expose P's aggregation equations, potential function, evaluator access, or convergence assumptions, nor the algebraic form of Q's nested cyclical monotonicity condition.  Calling MARS-RA itself a peer-scoring mechanism is therefore unsupported: its abstract describes comparisons generated by large multimodal models, not reports submitted by agents \ledger{3, 5}.

The model must still specify types, the feasible imitation relation, observations, the comparison oracle, timing, rewards, and the obedience action.  It is also unresolved whether a strategically affected potential retains the policy-invariance or convergence properties relevant to P.  Finally, satisfying an implementability condition and producing effective learned cooperation are different claims; neither abstract supports collapsing the formal and empirical questions \ledger{18, 22}.

\section{Smallest next step}

Obtain both full papers and instantiate one two-type, two-action external-oracle mechanism using P's actual comparison-to-score and shaping equations.  Then compute its induced allocation and test Q's nested cyclical monotonicity condition.  This single calculation determines whether there is a genuine counterexample before selecting a repair or running experiments \ledger{12, 19}.
