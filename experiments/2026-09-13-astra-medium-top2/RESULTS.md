# Astra medium pilot: highest scan scores

The two highest-ranked pairs, Q4P10 and Q3P9, both ended in PAUSE after one
research round. Both readable notes were produced successfully. The new
accounts contain more technical substance than their historical counterparts,
but do not establish the proposed empirical connexions.

The historical research outputs for these pairs were generated by GPT-5.6 Sol,
not Opus. This is a comparison with those recorded Sol outputs.

## Usage

The pilot ran on the ChatGPT Pro subscription with `gpt-6-astra`, reasoning
effort `medium`, and no fast-mode override. It used fresh ledgers, copied
sources and prompts, and a snapshot of the pipeline. It did not rerun scanning
or start paper authoring.

Per-pair session counts were: researchers 4; consolidation 1; verification 1;
editing 1. Approximately 91.3% of input tokens were cached.
Repeated contexts contribute to these token totals.

The subscription endpoint reported a weekly Codex window and no separate
five-hour window for this bucket. Its percentages have whole-point precision.
The account change also includes concurrent activity, including the supervising
conversation; it is not an exact attribution of two points to the child agents.
Receipt dollar values are unpriced placeholders, not measured charges.

Multiplying this observed change by seven projects about 14 percentage points
for 14 similar pairs. This is only a rough planning estimate: these runs ended
after one research round, whereas other pairs may iterate or require repairs.

## Quality comparison

| Pair | Historical Sol research rounds | Astra research rounds | Readable-note pages, Sol to Astra |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Q4P10 | 1 | 1 | 4 to 7 |
| Q3P9 | 2 | 1 | 4 to 6 |

Q4P10 shows the clearest improvement in technical specificity. The old note
extracted Q's scoring threshold and a generic bounded-incentive condition. The
new note supplies a score-reversal counterexample, a sharp peak-payment bound,
and a calculation showing why peak payout and expected spending differ. It
also checks score normalisation, audits the timing of energy deductions and
completed votes, and identifies the missing empirical test after conditioning
on public history. The verifier accepted the conditional calculations but
found no evidence that the proposed information loss occurs in P.

Q3P9 adds a qualified effective-price reduction, explicit feasible chip
exchanges, a correction to an unconditional reward bound, and examples
separating surplus from price convergence. The proposed points-contract
counterexample remains conditional on unavailable implementation semantics.
The experiment design distinguishes mistaken prohibitions from mistaken plan
sufficiency. The historical note retains a useful stopping-rule audit that
should not be discarded merely because the new run pursued another direction.

The Astra accounts are more substantial, but longer. They retain the original
limits on what can be concluded, and both say why empirical work is needed.
This is a qualitative assessment of two runs, not evidence of general model
superiority: sampling variation, historical prompt versions and a changed
verifier prevent a clean causal comparison.
