# What comparison accuracy can and cannot buy

Research note by emmy. Inputs are Q, *Mechanism Design for Alignment and Control*, and P, *MARS-RA*. This note
complements ada's participation-boundary analysis. It does not establish a bug in P's implementation or a new
general contract theorem.

## Assessment of the proposed connection

The abstract scan conflates statistical estimation with strategic implementation. P obtains comparisons from
an external LMM given egocentric images, task context, and agent identities (P, lines 280-310). Its actors do
not submit the comparison answers. Its convergence target is explicitly the judge's latent preference (P,
lines 361-386). Its Shapley interpretation assumes the latent preference already equals the desired Shapley
vector (P, lines 390-402). None supplies incentive compatibility.

Q makes agents' intrinsic utilities, private information, and feasible deviations explicit. Its IC condition
includes both a false report and a subsequent action deviation (Q, lines 372-396 and 1580-1620). A revelation
principle characterizes outcomes already achievable in equilibrium; it does not certify an arbitrary rank
aggregator. Q's peer discipline also needs separated conditional beliefs, cancellation of reported intrinsic
utility, and prohibited actions (Q, lines 1660-1725). P supplies none of those instruments.

The sharpened question is: **After separating learning advice from incentive-changing transfers, what evidence
and payment capacity would a strategic version of MARS-RA need to deter a profitable deviation?** This changes
the scan's question from justifying existing strategic robustness to identifying prerequisites for a new
mechanism.

There are distinct restrictions. With information and strategy spaces fixed and a pre-choice initial potential,
correctly accounted PBRS cannot change a player's preference between complete strategies. Even if a non-PBRS
contract is added, a comparison-only evaluator may lack enough information to
enforce obedience. Ada develops the first restriction and its participation-boundary exception. I develop the
second.

## A measurement restriction on Q's obedience condition

Fix a type, its report, a recommendation, and other agents' strategies. Compare the desired feasible action or
continuation strategy \(a^\star\) to a feasible deviation \(d\). Let the agent's expected intrinsic utility
gain from deviating be

\[
g=\mathbb E[u(d)]-\mathbb E[u(a^\star)]>0.
\]

Expectations use the agent's beliefs, as in Q. Let \(Z\) include **all** images, prompts, identity metadata,
and other evidence available to the judge. Denote its laws under the two strategies by \(\mu_\star\) and
\(\mu_d\). The repeated judge transcript \(Y_K\) is generated by a common stochastic kernel from \(Z\).
Queries may be adaptive, but must acquire no further evidence about the action beyond \(Z\). Let
\(\nu_\star^K\) and \(\nu_d^K\) denote the transcript laws.

Suppose a proposed extra transfer \(T(Y_K)\) has range at most \(B\). It is additive to intrinsic utility, and
there is no direct action prohibition or other payment channel. The desired action can be optimal only if

\[
g\leq \mathbb E_{\nu_\star^K}[T]-\mathbb E_{\nu_d^K}[T]
\leq B\operatorname{TV}(\nu_\star^K,\nu_d^K)
\leq B\operatorname{TV}(\mu_\star,\mu_d).
\]

Here \(\operatorname{TV}(p,q)=\tfrac12\sum_z|p(z)-q(z)|\) for finite spaces. To prove the middle inequality,
subtract the minimum transfer and divide by \(B\), obtaining a function taking values in \([0,1]\); its
expectation difference is bounded by TV. For the last inequality, condition the normalized transfer on \(Z\).
The resulting conditional expectation remains in \([0,1]\). This also proves the data-processing bound without
an independence assumption on the repeated answers.

For a single deviation and unrestricted transcript-dependent transfers in \([0,B]\), the first upper bound is
tight: pay \(B\) on the event where the desired transcript law exceeds the deviation law, and zero elsewhere.
Thus the minimum budget is \(g/\operatorname{TV}(\nu_\star^K,\nu_d^K)\) when TV is positive. Equality gives
weak obedience; strict obedience requires a strictly larger budget. Multiple deviations require a common
contract satisfying all their inequalities; pairwise bounds alone are then only necessary.

If \(\mu_\star=\mu_d\), every comparison-based payment has equal expectation under the two actions. A
profitable deviation survives every finite budget and every query count. Q already flags the general issue: “A
reward can respond only to what the evaluator can identify about the action and the state” (Q, line 917). P's
empirical discussion gives a relevant stress condition: simultaneous wall-facing views lack informative visual
references (P, lines 547-549 and 1080-1084). This motivates an experiment, but does not prove equality of
evidence laws in the benchmark.

This is an obedience obstruction with the report fixed. It therefore suffices to rule out full Q-style IC in
the specified case without inventing an agent-report channel for P. A general analysis of joint report/action
deviations must include reports among the observable payment inputs and specify their timing.

## Exact shared-image example

Consider a binary evidence summary \(Z\). Under the desired action, \(\Pr(Z=1)=3/5\); under a cheaper
deviation, \(\Pr(Z=1)=2/5\). Both actions remain feasible. Their intrinsic utility difference favors deviation
by \(g=3/10\).

Conditional on the image, each repeated pairwise judgment \(Y_k\) independently favors the agent with
probability

\[
\Pr(Y_k=1\mid Z=1)=9/10,
\qquad
\Pr(Y_k=1\mid Z=0)=1/10.
\]

This is an exact two-agent Bradley-Terry comparator for either fixed image: its score difference is
respectively \(\log 9\) or \(-\log 9\). Thus conditional ranking estimation can become arbitrarily accurate.
The evidence remains imperfect about which action produced it.

Writing \(N_K=\sum_{k=1}^K Y_k\), the desired and deviation transcript distributions reduce to mixtures of
binomial laws. Their total variation is exactly

\[
\operatorname{TV}(\nu_\star^K,\nu_d^K)
=\frac15\operatorname{TV}\!\left(
\operatorname{Bin}(K,9/10),\operatorname{Bin}(K,1/10)
\right)
\longrightarrow\frac15.
\]

Consequently, a transfer of range \(B=1\) cannot deter the deviation for any number of repeated judgments. The
minimum feasible budget approaches \(3/2\), exceeding the available budget. If instead the transfer pays the
empirical win fraction, its incentive advantage is exactly \(4/25\) at every query count. For two agents this
fraction is also the fitted softmax probability, using the limiting extension when the unregularized MLE
separates. Lower variance does not increase this expected incentive advantage.

Now acquire a fresh independent image before every judgment, keeping the same evidence model. The marginal win
probabilities become \(29/50\) and \(21/50\), and the comparison count laws become ordinary binomials with
these parameters. Their TV tends to one. A likelihood-ratio payment can now induce the desired action with the
budget \(B=1\) once enough views have been acquired.

| 1 | 0.160000 | 0.160000 | 1.875000 | 1.875000 |
| 4 | 0.188800 | 0.237952 | 1.588983 | 1.260758 |
| 8 | 0.198909 | 0.341177 | 1.508229 | 0.879310 |
| 16 | 0.199987 | 0.474047 | 1.500101 | 0.632849 |
| 64 | 0.200000 | 0.800612 | 1.500000 | 0.374714 |

These are exact finite-distribution calculations rounded for display, not MARS-Bench observations. The
fresh-view experiment changes the evidence acquisition process, which may have physical costs. It is not
equivalent to resampling an LMM on a frozen frame. The direct payment experiment changes P's objective and
must not be presented as its original PBRS.

Reproduction: `uv run --no-project emmy/check_information.py`. The script uses rational arithmetic and checks
normalization-derived TV identities, the optimal payment event, the information ceiling, and the constant
empirical-score payment gap. Numeric outputs are in `emmy/information_results.json`.

## Relation to ada's claims and a viable experiment

I checked ada's masked-PBRS identity directly. For a reward mask \(m_t\),

\[
\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\gamma^t m_t(\gamma\Phi_{t+1}-\Phi_t)
=-m_0\Phi_0+
\sum_{t=1}^{H-1}\gamma^t(m_{t-1}-m_t)\Phi_t
+\gamma^H m_{H-1}\Phi_H.
\]

With zero terminal and inactive potentials, re-entry can leave a negative potential debit. The formula is
correct, but P does not specify the necessary inactive-agent accounting details. The participation exploit is
conditional until code or trajectory returns establish how rewards are actually accumulated. The initial
potential must also be fixed before the strategic choice for ordinary invariance to hold. A report-dependent
initial potential acts as a report-dependent transfer.

Common latent-score translation is another possible evidence loss: P's pairwise probabilities and softmax are
invariant to it. However, losing a distinction matters for implementation only when the indistinguishable
cases require different incentives or behavior. Q's coupled reward example successfully uses action
differences because a known bias curve makes those differences identifying (Q, lines 1861-1909). Relative
information is not inherently inadequate.

The most coherent project is an **incentive audit of comparison-based shaping in open teams**, with ada's
boundary accounting as its main concrete target. The evidence calculation above is a restriction on proposed
repairs, not a competing generic mechanism. A small follow-up experiment can hold the desired/deviating
behaviors fixed, compare repeated frozen-frame judgments with genuinely new views, and measure the maximum
contract margin at a fixed payment budget. Include an oracle evidence condition to distinguish missing visual
information from judge error. Use Q's gain from a feasible deviation as the incentive metric, alongside P's
comparison accuracy. Success-rate improvements alone do not establish IC.

For a runnable embodied extension, introduce a documented private cost for contribution or for revealing a
useful view. This is a new strategic environment. Preserve P's original cooperative condition as a control.
Compare original PBRS, correctly accounted PBRS, and an explicit bounded contract plus PBRS. The explicit
contract changes incentives; PBRS can then guide learning under that contract. Full multi-type IC, unknown
utility gains, and the cost of active sensing remain unresolved.

## Prior work and publication judgment

The underlying mathematical obstructions are established techniques. [Devlin and Kudenko,
2012](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2012/papers/2C_3.pdf) prove dynamic PBRS preserves equilibrium
incentives. [Grzes, 2017](https://aamas.csc.liv.ac.uk/Proceedings/aamas2017/pdfs/p565.pdf) analyzes episodic
terminal terms and their correction. [Saig, Einav, and Talgam-Cohen, 2024, Proposition
1](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/5b93ce41ac6de2bf9aca7e4ba5ba01d5-Paper-Conference.pdf)
gives a stronger minimum-budget characterization via TV distance to mixtures of deviations. Our two-action
contract bound is a specialization of that result.

Searches run: `potential based reward shaping multi agent dynamic population agent death policy invariance`;
`moral hazard bounded payments total variation informativeness monitoring signals`; `contract design "total
variation" bounded payments`; `moral hazard "statistical tests" optimal contracts bounded`; `reward shaping
dynamic agents terminal states episodic policy invariance Grzes 2017`; and `"Incentivizing Quality Text
Generation via Statistical"`.

There is a concrete research question, but not yet a publication-level result. The likely contribution would
be a demonstrated participation-accounting failure and repair in P's distinctive open-team setting, with
carefully separated information and incentive tests. The current derivations justify that audit. They do not
justify advertising a new revelation principle, strategic robustness of MARS-RA, or novelty for the generic TV
bound. If P's implementation already settles boundary terms correctly and the empirical evidence intervention
adds little beyond known statistical-contract results, the appropriate verdict is that this pair has yielded a
useful clarification rather than a paper.
