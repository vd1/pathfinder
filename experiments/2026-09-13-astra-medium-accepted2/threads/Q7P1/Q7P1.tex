\section*{The pair in two sentences}
Q, \emph{A Systematic Approach to Mechanism Design with Stochastic Dynamic
Stability}, proposes quadratic payments for strategic resource allocation,
claiming welfare implementation, budget balance, individual rationality, and
mean-square convergence of variable sample-size proximal best responses.
P, \emph{Distributed risk-averse optimization via CVaR}, supplies empirical
conditional value-at-risk (CVaR) values and a one-point gradient estimator for
cooperative optimization, whose fixed-batch limit optimizes an expected
empirical smoothed surrogate (ledger entries 3, 13, 15, 19).

\section*{The connexion pursued}
The initial connexion was to use P's value oracle in Q's strategic learning
scheme. The supported replacement is narrower: correct Q's payments, implement
a fixed risk surrogate, and quantify the difference from population-risk
incentives. A weighted proximal-point learner replaces Q's original iteration
(entries 15, 17, 19, 24, 26). This article consolidates the ledger through entry
27 and both peers' notes; the allowance ended without a final common readiness
checkpoint covering every last entry. Earlier readiness declarations are not
treated as approval of later material.

\section*{What was found and why}
\paragraph{An obstruction preceding CVaR.}
Emmy and Ada independently found that Q's implementation equality P2(i)
contradicts its strict LMI P1(ii) (entries 1--3). For a nonzero resource vector
\(v\), put \(h=\operatorname{col}((0,v),\ldots,(0,v))\). Q's price blocks give
\[
 h^\top(\Psi+\Psi^\top)h
 =-2\sum_n v^\top\left(\sum_m A_{nm}^n\right)v=0,
\]
whereas the strict certificate requires a value at most
\(-\upsilon N\|v\|^2<0\). This disproves simultaneous feasibility of the
stated certificates, not existence of equilibria for every mechanism.

\paragraph{A payment failure and its repair.}
Write \(\gamma>0\) for Q's tax scale and
\(b_N=(N^2-N+1)/(N-1)^2\), with \(N\ge2\). Direct collection of Q's
equation \texttt{eq40}, at common prices and complementary capacity, gives
\[
 t_n^Q=\gamma b_N p^\top(x_n-c/N).
\]
The printed coefficients also give
\(\sum_m a_m^n=\gamma c/[N(N-1)]\), violating Q's P4(ii) for positive
capacity (entries 5--7). Ada's counterexample takes \(N=3\),
\(\gamma=c=1\), \(X_n=[0,1]\), and
\[
 V_1(x)=10x-x^2/2,\qquad V_2(x)=V_3(x)=0.1x-x^2/2.
\]
At \(x=(1,0,0)\), \(p=(9,9,9)\), own price derivatives vanish and own
allocation cost derivatives are \(0,8.9,8.9\), with the latter at lower
bounds. Each own cost Hessian is
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1/2\\-1/2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\succ0\),
so these KKT conditions suffice for a Nash equilibrium. Nevertheless,
\(t_1^Q=10.5>V_1(1)=9.5\): individual rationality fails. Emmy checked the
same failure with coefficient \(1\) instead of \(0.1\) for the other
valuations (entries 6, 7, 9).

Ada's opponent-only adjustment, confirmed by Emmy, is
\begin{align*}
 t_n^{\rm corr}&=t_n^Q+r_n,\\
 r_n&=\gamma(b_N-1)(\bar p_{-n}^{\rm av})^\top
 \left(\sum_{j\ne n}x_j-\frac{N-1}{N}c\right),\qquad
 \bar p_{-n}^{\rm av}=\frac{\sum_{j\ne n}p_j}{N-1}.
\end{align*}
It preserves all best responses. At common complementary prices,
\(t_n^{\rm corr}=\lambda^\top(x_n-c/N)\), with \(\lambda=\gamma p\),
so equilibrium budget balance is exact. For normalized concave valuations,
\(0\in X_n\), nonnegative capacity and multipliers, planner stationarity
and the supporting-gradient inequality give
\[
 V_n(x_n)\ge\lambda^\top x_n,\qquad
 V_n(x_n)-t_n^{\rm corr}\ge\lambda^\top c/N\ge0.
\]
Thus the repair gives individual rationality when implementation holds;
it does not restore Q's coefficient certificate or assert budget balance
away from equilibrium (entries 8, 9, 17, 19).

\paragraph{Implementation without unique prices.}
Emmy's proof uses compact convex local sets, strongly convex costs
\(f_n=-V_n\), valid subgradient/normal-cone sum rules, and existence of a
planner KKT pair. Let \(m\) be a common curvature lower bound,
\(\Pi=\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/N\), \(\Delta=I-\Pi\), and
\(\kappa=(2N-1)/(N-1)^2\); matrices act resourcewise.
Differentiating \texttt{eq40} and scaling price rows by \((N-1)/N\) gives
\[
 G_x\in\partial f(x)+\gamma(\Pi-\kappa\Delta)p,
 \qquad G_p=\gamma\Delta p-\gamma\Pi x+\frac{\gamma}{N}\mathbf1c.
\]
For differences \(u=x-x'\), \(v=p-p'\), completion of squares yields
\begin{align*}
 \langle G(s)-G(s'),s-s'\rangle
 &\ge m\|\Pi u\|^2
 +(m-\gamma\kappa^2/4)\|\Delta u\|^2\\
 &\quad+\gamma\|\Delta v-(\kappa/2)\Delta u\|^2.
\end{align*}
Consequently the sufficient threshold is
\[
 m>\frac{\gamma(2N-1)^2}{4(N-1)^4}.
\]
It also ensures own joint-cost convexity. A planner KKT pair gives a Nash
equilibrium at \(p_n=\lambda/\gamma\). Adding its variational inequality
to that of any other equilibrium forces \(u=0\) and \(\Delta v=0\).
The remaining stationarity conditions identify common prices as planner
multipliers. Thus allocations are unique, while prices need not be
(entries 4, 9, 14, 17, 19; Emmy's implementation proof).

\paragraph{The statistical surrogate and economic errors.}
Use upper-tail probability \(\beta_n\), bounded losses \(|J_n|\le U_n\),
samplewise convexity, Lipschitz constants \(L_n\), and fresh iid samples
from a query-independent distribution. Set
\[
 C_n=\operatorname{CVaR}_{\beta_n}(J_n),\quad
 H_{n,s}=\operatorname{E}\widehat C_{n,s},\quad
 e_n=\frac{U_n}{\beta_n}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{s_n}}.
\]
The threshold representation gives
\(\operatorname{E}\inf_\tau\widehat\phi\le
\inf_\tau\operatorname{E}\widehat\phi\), hence \(H_{n,s}\le C_n\).
P's integrated Dvoretzky--Kiefer--Wolfowitz bound gives the other side,
\(C_n-e_n\le H_{n,s}\). Centered ball smoothing and Jensen's inequality
therefore imply
\[
 -e_n\le\widetilde C_n(x)-C_n(x)\le L_n\delta_n,
 \qquad w_n=e_n+L_n\delta_n.
\]
These are bounds between deterministic expected functions, requiring no
expected supremum of empirical errors (Ada, entry 13; Emmy's check, entry 17).

Normalize true and surrogate valuations at zero:
\(V_n(x)=C_n(0)-C_n(x)\) and analogously for \(\widetilde V_n\).
For satisfaction data use loss \(J_n=-\psi_n\); deterministic taxes enter
additively by cash translation. At a corrected surrogate equilibrium, the
oscillation \(w_n\) bounds actual unilateral regret and individual-rationality
shortfall. Comparing surrogate optimality at \(\widetilde x\) and a true
optimum \(x^*\) gives
\[
 \sum_n C_n(\widetilde x_n)-\sum_n C_n(x_n^*)\le\sum_n w_n,
 \qquad
 \|\widetilde x-x^*\|^2\le\frac{2\sum_n w_n}{m_0},
\]
when total population cost has curvature \(m_0>0\). Budget balance stays
exact. Applied to P with its additional domain-comparator term, the same
value argument gives limiting error of order
\(O(\delta_{\max}+s_{\min}^{-1/2})\); it does not eliminate inverse-radius
variance in finite-time learning (entries 13, 17 and Ada's note).

\paragraph{Local queries preserving zero.}
The late continuation resolves the local boundary objection. Given a known
relative interior ball \(a_n+\rho_n\mathcal B\subset X_n\), query at
\[
 q_n(x,u)=(1-\varepsilon_n)x+\varepsilon_na_n+\delta_nu,
 \qquad 0<\delta_n<\varepsilon_n\rho_n.
\]
This is a convex combination of \(x\) and an interior-ball point, so every
query is locally feasible. Decisions still include zero. For ball direction
\(v\), define \(K_n(x)=\operatorname{E}\widehat C_{n,s}(q_n(x,v))\).
P's sphere identity and the chain rule give the unbiased oracle
\[
 \widehat g_n^K(x)=\frac{(1-\varepsilon_n)d_n}{\delta_n}
 \widehat C_{n,s}(q_n(x,u))u.
\]
Here directions and dimension are relative to the local affine hull.
Samplewise curvature \(m_n\) becomes \(m_n(1-\varepsilon_n)^2\), whose
minimum must exceed the weighted threshold above. With
\(R_n=\sup_{x\in X_n}\|x-a_n\|\), the error interval is
\[
 -e_n-L_n\varepsilon_nR_n\le K_n-C_n
 \le L_n\delta_n+L_n\varepsilon_nR_n.
\]
All economic bounds hold with
\(w_n^K=e_n+2L_n\varepsilon_nR_n+L_n\delta_n\). Taking
\(\delta_n=\varepsilon_n\rho_n/2\) gives order
\(O(s_n^{-1/2}+\varepsilon_n)\). This requires fresh samples at arbitrary
locally feasible queries and guarantees no joint capacity feasibility of
experiments (Ada, entries 20, 24; Emmy, entries 22, 26).

\paragraph{A changed learner with a proof.}
For fixed surrogate parameters, assume a compact product strategy set
\(\mathcal S\) with a price box containing a surrogate planner KKT
equilibrium. The preceding comparison proves that all boxed equilibria
retain the implemented allocation and complementary common prices; the
reference price need not be strictly inside the box (entries 17, 24).
With normal cone \(N_{\mathcal S}\), use
\[
 R(z)=(I+\eta(\widetilde G+N_{\mathcal S}))^{-1}z,\qquad\eta>0.
\]
The inner operator \(\widetilde G(s)+\eta^{-1}(s-z)\) is strongly
monotone. P's oracle, analytic payment gradients, projection, and aggregate
messages give, for appropriate diminishing inner steps,
\(\operatorname{E}[\|s_T-R(z)\|^2\mid z]\le C/(T+1)\).
Taking \(T_k=\lceil(k+1)^{2+\zeta}\rceil\), \(\zeta>0\), makes expected
norm errors summable, hence almost surely summable. Firm nonexpansiveness
of the resolvent gives convergent distances and vanishing residual;
compactness identifies an equilibrium limit. Boundedness then gives
mean-square convergence, with deterministic limiting allocation and possibly
random common price (entries 11, 17, 19, 24; Emmy's full recurrence proof).

\section*{Objections and their disposition}
The LMI contradiction and original individual-rationality failure remain
objections to Q as printed; the replacement arguments do not validate those
claims. Nor does weighted monotonicity prove Q's original proximal
best-response (PBR) iteration nonexpansive. Emmy's interior quadratic example
with \(N=3\), \(m=\gamma=1\) has consensus-price column
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-(\mu+1)/D\\1-1/(2D)\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 D=(\mu+1)^2-1/4,
\]
whose squared norm is \(1+1/(2D^2)>1\) for every \(\mu>0\).
Ada independently checked it. This refutes automatic Euclidean
nonexpansiveness, not every possible convergence proof (entries 10, 14).

Expected curvature in Q does not ensure CVaR curvature; samplewise strong
convexity is an added assumption (entries 9, 19). Full-message uniqueness
also fails: with \(N=3\), \(X_n=[0,1]\), \(c=3/2\), \(\gamma=1\),
equiprobable \(Z=\pm1\), and tail mass \(1/2\),
\[
 J_n(x,Z)=x^2-3x+Z(x-1/2),\qquad
 C_n(x)=x^2-3x+|x-1/2|.
\]
The unique allocation is \(x_n=1/2\), but
\(\partial C_n(1/2)=[-3,-1]\), allowing every common price in \([1,3]\)
(entries 14, 17). The robust conclusion is allocation convergence.
The boundary objection is resolved only locally under the known-ball oracle
model, as the final checks explicitly stress (entries 24, 26).

\section*{What remains open}
The evidence for the algebraic corrections and conditional fixed-surrogate
theorem is substantive, with independent exact-arithmetic checks recorded in
entries 14, 17, 24, and 26. The material is thin on publication novelty and
practical efficiency: no complete closest-literature comparison, efficient
total oracle complexity, or stochastic performance benchmark is supplied.
Convergence of original VS-PBR, an exact population-risk schedule with changing
batches and radii, and jointly capacity-feasible exploratory queries remain
unproved (entry 26). Fixed-surrogate convergence does not settle them.

\section*{The smallest next step}
For the immediate publication question, read the closest full papers listed
above and compare their assumptions and conclusions against the corrected
payment formula, exact equilibrium transfer properties, and oscillation-based
economic errors. This is the smallest missing check identified by both notes
that could establish whether the supported package adds a distinct result
(entries 15, 19, 26). It would settle that narrower novelty comparison;
the separate algorithmic and joint-query questions still require their own
proofs, and the record supplies no single step that settles all of them.
