\begin{abstract}
Concept-guided robot perception can use an estimated room to restrict later door detection, but a hard geometric threshold can turn a small wall error into a changed decision. We isolate the first wall-distance gate of the concept-guided exploration system of Zapata et al. and connect it to the interface and calibration requirements identified by Zhao et al. % Ledger: 8, 12, 30
For a frozen room fitter, exchangeable ground-truthed calibration episodes give a split-conformal upper radius for Hausdorff wall-set error. % Ledger: 23, 25, 34
On the resulting room-level coverage event, the distance-to-set inequality certifies, simultaneously, every first-gate decision outside an explicit ambiguity band. % Ledger: 23, 25, 26, 34
The probability statement is marginal over rooms, applies only to the first gate, and does not certify later door detection, fallback accuracy, or robot motion. % Ledger: 24, 31, 35
No episode-level calibration data are available in the source papers, so this is a theorem and validation protocol rather than a demonstrated calibrated robot system. % Ledger: 35
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Learning-augmented algorithms distinguish performance with useful and arbitrary predictions and require explicit accounting when guarantees pass between components \cite{zhao2026laa}. % Ledger: 5, 6, 30
Concept-guided exploration supplies a concrete, discontinuous interface: an estimated room wall set constrains a later door detector \cite{zapata2026concept}. % Ledger: 8, 30
The detector retains a scan point $p$ when
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{dist}(p,W)\leq\delta, \qquad \delta=0.15\,\mathrm{m},
  \label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
before constructing a polar scan, differentiating it, and pairing peaks. % Ledger: 8, 13, 30
Zapata et al. report that noise can lead to accepted incorrect room models, while revision of accepted models is left for future work. % Ledger: 5, 6, 22, 30

This paper asks a deliberately narrower question: can the first gate in \eqref{eq:gate} abstain selectively with a finite-sample statistical certificate? % Ledger: 21, 30
Our new result is a reduction from marginal, room-level Hausdorff coverage to simultaneous correctness of every non-abstained first-gate decision in a new exchangeable room. % Ledger: 23, 25, 26, 34
The reduction combines a standard split-conformal rank argument with the distance-to-set inequality; neither source paper states this problem-specific chain. % Ledger: 25, 26, 30
The claim is weaker than full selective safety for door perception: later processing couples scan points and remains uncertified. % Ledger: 13, 14, 31

The paper keeps four categories separate. Section~\ref{sec:sources} records what the two source papers establish. Section~\ref{sec:result} gives the new result. Section~\ref{sec:interpretation} offers interpretation and a validation protocol, not further theorems. Sections~\ref{sec:limitations} and~\ref{sec:open} state limitations and open questions.

\section{Calibrated selective gate}
\label{sec:result}

Let $W^*$ and $\widehat W$ be non-empty wall sets in the same metric space, using the same convention for wall centreline or surface, spatial extent, and reference frame. % Ledger: 28, 31, 34
Write
\[
  d_H(\widehat W,W^*)
\]
for their Hausdorff distance. For every point $p$,
\begin{equation}
 \left|\operatorname{dist}(p,\widehat W)-\operatorname{dist}(p,W^*)\right|
 \leq d_H(\widehat W,W^*).
 \label{eq:setdistance}
\end{equation}
% Ledger: 8, 12, 23, 25

\begin{proposition}[Deterministic margin transfer]
If $d_H(\widehat W,W^*)\leq\varepsilon$, then
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{dist}(p,\widehat W)+\varepsilon\leq\delta
 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{dist}(p,W^*)\leq\delta, \label{eq:accept}\\
 \operatorname{dist}(p,\widehat W)-\varepsilon>\delta
 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{dist}(p,W^*)>\delta. \label{eq:reject}
\end{align}
Points satisfying neither antecedent are called ambiguous.
\end{proposition}
% Ledger: 8, 12, 23, 25

\begin{proof}
Both implications follow directly from \eqref{eq:setdistance}. The weak inequality in \eqref{eq:accept} and strict inequality in \eqref{eq:reject} match P's acceptance convention in \eqref{eq:gate}.
\end{proof}
% Ledger: 12

Without a positive margin from the threshold, gate membership is unstable under arbitrarily small wall displacement. % Ledger: 8, 12
For any positive displacement, a point can be placed sufficiently close to the threshold so that translating a wall by no more than that displacement flips the binary gate value. % Ledger: 8
Thus the binary gate is not globally Lipschitz as a map from Hausdorff wall error to membership error. % Ledger: 8, 12

The deterministic statement is not operational until $\varepsilon$ is obtained. Freeze the complete room fitter independently of calibration. % Ledger: 25, 34
For $n$ exchangeable, ground-truthed calibration episodes, define
\begin{equation}
 R_i=d_H(\widehat W_i,W_i^*), \qquad
 k=\left\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\right\rceil .
 \label{eq:scores}
\end{equation}
% Ledger: 23, 25, 34
Let $\varepsilon_\alpha$ be the $k$th order statistic of $R_1,\ldots,R_n$, with $\varepsilon_\alpha=+\infty$ if $k>n$. % Ledger: 23, 25, 34

\begin{theorem}[Marginal simultaneous first-gate certificate]
Suppose the frozen fitter is applied to calibration episodes and one new episode such that their scores are exchangeable. Then
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\!\left\{d_H(\widehat W_{\mathrm{new}},W^*_{\mathrm{new}})
 \leq\varepsilon_\alpha\right\}\geq 1-\alpha.
 \label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}
On the event inside \eqref{eq:coverage}, every new-room point certified by \eqref{eq:accept} or \eqref{eq:reject}, using $\varepsilon_\alpha$, receives the same first-gate decision as it would under $W^*_{\mathrm{new}}$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \Pr\!\left\{\substack{\text{all non-ambiguous first-gate decisions agree}\\
 \text{with the reference-wall gate}}\right\}
 \geq 1-\alpha.
 \label{eq:simultaneous}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
% Ledger: 23, 25, 26, 34

\begin{proof}
Exchangeability makes the new score's rank among the $n+1$ scores uniform up to conservative handling of ties. The order-statistic choice in \eqref{eq:scores}, including the infinite-radius convention, gives \eqref{eq:coverage}. On that single room-level event, \eqref{eq:setdistance} holds for every scan point. The deterministic margin transfer then proves all certified decisions simultaneously. No union bound over scan points is used.
\end{proof}
% Ledger: 23, 25, 26, 34

Equation~\eqref{eq:simultaneous} is marginal over exchangeable episodes. It is not conditional coverage for a given room, geometry class, or noise regime. % Ledger: 24, 26, 31

\section{Interpretation and validation protocol}
\label{sec:interpretation}

The theorem suggests an interface contract rather than an end-to-end robot guarantee. Outside the ambiguity band, estimated-wall processing agrees with the reference-wall first gate on the covered room event. Inside the band, a system must abstain or invoke a separately justified procedure. % Ledger: 12, 15, 30
This is an interpretation of the theorem, not evidence that any particular fallback is accurate or efficient. % Ledger: 9, 31

Pointwise fallback is not supported. Removing a point changes the polar scan's adjacency and can alter derivatives and peak pairs, so a fallback must cover a whole scan or a declared dependency region with fixed grid, interpolation, and boundary rules. % Ledger: 14, 15, 18, 30
No such regional contract is implemented or evaluated in the supplied material. % Ledger: 31, 35

The smallest informative experiment is to predeclare a deployment population and wall-set convention, freeze P's fitter, and collect disjoint ground-truthed calibration and test rooms in its simulator. % Ledger: 26, 30, 34, 35
It should report test-room coverage, $\varepsilon_\alpha/\delta$, and the fraction of declared dependency regions sent to fallback, alongside compute and motion as separate quantities, door recall, and topology error. % Ledger: 24, 28, 30, 35
An outcome with $\varepsilon_\alpha\geq0.15\,\mathrm{m}$ or near-total regional fallback is a valid but operationally negative result. % Ledger: 24, 28, 35

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The theorem has the following boundaries.
\begin{itemize}
 \item Exchangeability is essential. Distribution shift, a new building or sensor regime, and dependent adaptive sampling are not covered. % Ledger: 24, 31
 \item Coverage is marginal over rooms, not conditional safety for an individual room or rare layout. % Ledger: 24, 26, 31
 \item Estimated and reference walls must share a wall-set convention, extent, and frame. Otherwise the calibration score is not the claimed quantity. % Ledger: 28, 31, 34
 \item Only the first distance gate is certified. Derivative, peak-pair, width, segment, final door, and topology decisions are not certified. % Ledger: 9, 13, 31, 35
 \item The result does not establish fallback correctness or cost, and it does not establish a competitive guarantee for policy-dependent robot motion. % Ledger: 4, 9, 31
 \item The source material contains no calibration episodes. Thus the radius,
 achieved coverage, and abstention cost cannot be computed here. % Ledger: 35
 \item The external prior-work search could not be completed because scholarly landing pages were unreachable, so literature-level novelty remains unresolved.
\end{itemize}

These limitations prevent the phrases ``safe door detector'' and ``calibrated robot system'' from being justified by the present result. % Ledger: 31, 35

\section{Open questions}
\label{sec:open}

The first open question is whether a defensible deployment population and independent episode unit can be specified for P's active exploration process. % Ledger: 26, 31
The second is empirical non-vacuity: Q and P do not show whether the calibrated radius is below the gate threshold or whether dependency expansion forces nearly total fallback. % Ledger: 28, 35
The third is how to define and validate a regional fallback whose own accuracy and work are independently characterised. % Ledger: 9, 14, 18, 31
The fourth is whether later discontinuities admit compatible margin conditions without strengthening the present first-gate theorem. % Ledger: 13, 31
Finally, a complete external literature search is needed to determine whether the geometric coverage-to-gate reduction or its main proof step has already been published.

\section{Conclusion}

The hard wall gate in P does not automatically preserve small geometric errors, but it admits a precise selective certificate. % Ledger: 8, 12
Under a frozen fitter, exchangeable calibration, and a fixed wall-set convention, a split-conformal Hausdorff radius gives marginal room-level coverage; on that event, the distance-to-set inequality transfers coverage simultaneously to every non-abstained first-gate decision. % Ledger: 23, 25, 26, 34
This is a first-interface theorem and validation protocol, not a claim about complete door perception or robot safety. % Ledger: 31, 35
