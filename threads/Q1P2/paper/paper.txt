A Calibrated Selective Certificate for a
Wall-Constrained Perception Gate
Anonymous
Abstract
Concept-guided robot perception can use an estimated room to restrict later door detection, but a hard geometric threshold can turn a
small wall error into a changed decision. We isolate the first wall-distance
gate of the concept-guided exploration system of Zapata et al. and connect it to the interface and calibration requirements identified by Zhao
et al. For a frozen room fitter, exchangeable ground-truthed calibration
episodes give a split-conformal upper radius for Hausdorff wall-set error.
On the resulting room-level coverage event, the distance-to-set inequality
certifies, simultaneously, every first-gate decision outside an explicit ambiguity band. The probability statement is marginal over rooms, applies
only to the first gate, and does not certify later door detection, fallback
accuracy, or robot motion. No episode-level calibration data are available
in the source papers, so this is a theorem and validation protocol rather
than a demonstrated calibrated robot system.

Interpretation and validation protocol

The theorem suggests an interface contract rather than an end-to-end robot
guarantee. Outside the ambiguity band, estimated-wall processing agrees with
the reference-wall first gate on the covered room event. Inside the band, a system
must abstain or invoke a separately justified procedure. This is an interpretation
of the theorem, not evidence that any particular fallback is accurate or efficient.
Pointwise fallback is not supported. Removing a point changes the polar
scan’s adjacency and can alter derivatives and peak pairs, so a fallback must
cover a whole scan or a declared dependency region with fixed grid, interpolation, and boundary rules. No such regional contract is implemented or evaluated
in the supplied material.
The smallest informative experiment is to predeclare a deployment population and wall-set convention, freeze P’s fitter, and collect disjoint ground-truthed
calibration and test rooms in its simulator. It should report test-room coverage, εα /δ, and the fraction of declared dependency regions sent to fallback,
alongside compute and motion as separate quantities, door recall, and topology
error. An outcome with εα ≥ 0.15 m or near-total regional fallback is a valid
but operationally negative result.

Limitations

The theorem has the following boundaries.
• Exchangeability is essential. Distribution shift, a new building or sensor
regime, and dependent adaptive sampling are not covered.
• Coverage is marginal over rooms, not conditional safety for an individual
room or rare layout.
• Estimated and reference walls must share a wall-set convention, extent,
and frame. Otherwise the calibration score is not the claimed quantity.
• Only the first distance gate is certified. Derivative, peak-pair, width,
segment, final door, and topology decisions are not certified.
• The result does not establish fallback correctness or cost, and it does not
establish a competitive guarantee for policy-dependent robot motion.
• The source material contains no calibration episodes, so neither εα , achieved
coverage, nor abstention cost can be computed here.

Open questions

The first open question is whether a defensible deployment population and independent episode unit can be specified for P’s active exploration process. The
second is empirical non-vacuity: Q and P do not show whether the calibrated radius is below the gate threshold or whether dependency expansion forces nearly
total fallback. The third is how to define and validate a regional fallback whose
own accuracy and work are independently characterised. The fourth is whether
later discontinuities admit compatible margin conditions without strengthening
the present first-gate theorem. Finally, a complete external literature search
is needed to determine whether the geometric coverage-to-gate reduction or its
main proof step has already been published.

8

Conclusion

The hard wall gate in P does not automatically preserve small geometric errors,
but it admits a precise selective certificate. Under a frozen fitter, exchangeable calibration, and a fixed wall-set convention, a split-conformal Hausdorff
radius gives marginal room-level coverage; on that event, the distance-to-set
inequality transfers coverage simultaneously to every non-abstained first-gate
decision. This is a first-interface theorem and validation protocol, not a claim
about complete door perception or robot safety.
