## What was read and what changed

The full Lu, Schwartz, and Givigi paper was downloaded from the Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research and read. It proves that potential-based shaping preserves Nash equilibria in multi-player general-sum stochastic games. It also states that similar work by Devlin and Kudenko appeared while that paper was under review. Therefore the broad Nash-invariance step and its potential-shaping rationale are treated as published prior work, not as this paper's contribution.

The search did not locate a verified publication stating the narrower construction used here: embed active-coalition softmax credits in a fixed population space, keep inactive coordinates at zero, and retain each population member's shaping ledger on every transition so that entry and exit do not break telescoping. The paper describes this only as the outcome of the recorded search, not as proof of novelty.

Bibliographic metadata for Q and P was checked against the arXiv API abstract records. Metadata for Lu, Schwartz, and Givigi was checked against the DOI/Crossref record and the journal PDF. No unverified search result was added to the bibliography.

## Round 4 search update

Searches were run on 13 September 2026, after review finding F1 (scalarisation scope). The aim was to check whether the two remaining steps of the result, invariance for time-varying comparison outputs and the entry and exit boundary terms, are already published.

- Web search: `dynamic potential-based reward shaping Devlin Kudenko time-varying potential Nash equilibria`. Returned Devlin and Kudenko, "Dynamic Potential-Based Reward Shaping", AAMAS 2012, pp. 433 to 440 (ACM, White Rose, York records), and an empirical multi-agent shaping study in Advances in Complex Systems. The author-deposited PDF (White Rose eprint 75121) was read. It defines F(s,t,s',t') = γΦ(s',t') − Φ(s,t), shows that along a fixed sequence the shaped return equals the unshaped return minus Φ(s_0,t_0), and concludes that policy invariance and consistent Nash equilibria survive time-varying potentials. P cites this paper for its own shaping. Change: the paper now states that the "arbitrary realised comparison outputs" clause of its theorem is covered by this prior work.
- Web search: `potential-based reward shaping agents entering and leaving open multi-agent reinforcement learning`. Returned general multi-agent shaping work (AAMAS 2025 PBRS effectiveness paper, a York thesis, logic-based shaping arXiv 2206.08881, shaping advice arXiv 2103.15941, a reward-engineering survey arXiv 2408.10215, vision-based potentials arXiv 2502.13430, SAT-MARL arXiv 2012.07949, decentralised shaping limits arXiv 2511.00034). None of the titles or summaries concerns agents entering or leaving. No change.
- Web search: `"reward shaping" "variable number of agents" potential-based credit assignment policy invariance`. Returned arXiv 2305.18380 (potential-based credit assignment for autonomous-vehicle testing), arXiv 2412.14779 (agent-temporal credit assignment, TAR²), arXiv 1401.3907 (the arXiv version of Lu, Schwartz, and Givigi) and broader items. The abstract pages of 2305.18380, 2412.14779 and 2511.00034 were read; none addresses changing coalitions, inactive agents, or per-agent terminal potentials. Change: the arXiv abstract page of 1401.3907 was checked (title, authors, 2011 journal reference, DOI 10.1613/jair.3384) and the identifier was added to the existing bibliography entry.
- Web search: `Grzes "Reward shaping in episodic reinforcement learning" terminal state potential zero policy invariance`. Returned Grześ, "Reward Shaping in Episodic Reinforcement Learning", AAMAS 2017 (ACM record, Kent and Semantic Scholar PDFs). The author PDF from the University of Kent was read. It derives U_Φ = U + γ^N Φ(s_N) − Φ(s_0) for trajectories that stop at s_N, notes that the γ^N Φ(s_N) term depends on actions when termination does, gives single-agent and two-agent coordination-game examples in which a nonzero terminal potential changes the policy or the set of Nash equilibria, and requires Φ(s_N) = 0. Change: the paper now states that the exit boundary term is a term of this published kind, and restricts its own addition to placing such terms at individual entry and exit under changing coalitions.
