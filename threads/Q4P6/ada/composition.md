# Incentive-Compatible Rank Aggregation for Open MARL

## Correction to the proposed connection

The scan's claim is false as stated. MARS-RA does not ask acting agents to report capability or contribution. An external LMM maps two egocentric observations to a comparison, `f_LMM(o_i, o_j, Theta)` (P, lines 281-303). Q's peer mechanism instead scores an agent's own type report against co-player reports and requires distinct types to induce distinct conditional distributions over peer reports (Q, lines 1650-1728). Revelation therefore gives no robustness guarantee for P's existing pipeline.

P's robustness is statistical, not incentive-theoretic. Its estimator converges to the LMM's stable latent preference under a Bradley-Terry model and graph connectivity (P, lines 358-376). The identification of that preference with contribution is assumed: the Shapley proposition begins with `c* = v*` and explicitly disclaims practical recovery (P, lines 380-389). More queries can consistently recover a biased or gamed referee target.

## Preferred joint advance

Replace the single trusted LMM referee with strategic, privately informed peer comparators. Each embodied agent, or an onboard evaluator attached to it, observes a partial view and submits pairwise contribution judgments. Score each report against correlated reports from other peers using Q's quadratic peer score, then aggregate the elicited comparisons with P's Bradley-Terry estimator, and use P's potential-based shaping stage.

This suggests a compositional result:

1. Under Q's finite-support belief closure and separation condition, and with transfers large enough relative to utility gains from lying, truthful peer reports are incentive compatible (Q, lines 1650-1728).
2. Conditional on truthful reports generated by a Bradley-Terry latent contribution vector and a connected comparison graph, P's MLE error is O(sqrt(n log n / K)) (P, lines 358-376).
3. With a valid state-time potential and zero terminal potential, the shaping rewards telescope, so the team-optimal policy is preserved while truthful aggregate feedback can alter learning speed (P, lines 331-349).

The resulting theorem would separate incentive error from statistical error. With exact incentive compatibility the latter decays with K. With epsilon-incentive compatibility, a useful new target is a bound of the schematic form estimation error <= statistical term + manipulation term depending on epsilon and graph connectivity.

This is not yet a composition of the papers' theorems. The missing centerpiece is an interface theorem. Truthful partial-view reports may be heterogeneous and correlated, while P assumes observations generated from one Bradley-Terry score vector. A publishable result must specify or learn a calibrated signal model, prove when its truthful reports identify a common latent ranking, and bound the effects of approximate incentives, misspecification, graph connectivity, and churn. Under exact calibration and connectedness, P's rate is a baseline corollary; the new value lies in the nonideal bound.

## Concrete experiment

MARS-Bench is unusually suitable because views are partial, team membership changes, and dense environment-internal rewards exist for evaluation only (P, lines 427 and 665). Compare a trusted centralized LMM, unscored strategic peer evaluators, peer-scored evaluators, and an oracle dense-reward comparator. Train evaluators with an explicit private objective that rewards inflating their attached agent's credit. Measure report truthfulness against the hidden dense reward, BT score error, team success, and robustness as agents enter or leave.

## Key objections and boundary conditions

- Q's theorem assumes the designer knows each type's conditional belief map, permits effectively unbounded penalties, and obtains support-wise implementation. These are strong assumptions, not a ready-made practical protocol.
- Peer prediction can have uninformative or coordinated equilibria. Q proves truth is optimal under its construction, not unique equilibrium selection.
- Contribution signals from partial views must be correlated and separated. Dynamic departure can disconnect the report graph or destroy peer availability exactly when evaluation is most needed.
- Actor-side visual gaming is not by itself a clean equilibrium claim. P's correctly implemented potential shaping telescopes and preserves optimal policies, so visual gaming is primarily a finite-training or implementation failure unless the potential assumptions fail. This limits the alternative proposal in ledger entry 3.

## Sharpened question

Changed from "does Q explain why current MARS-RA resists strategic agents?" to: "Can peer scoring make decentralized contribution comparisons incentive compatible, while rank aggregation makes them statistically efficient, in open partially observed MARL, and what error remains under approximate incentive compatibility and agent churn?"

Unresolved: whether the conditional-belief separation condition is empirically plausible in MARS-Bench, how to obtain or learn the belief map without defeating scalability, and whether truthful equilibrium can be selected against collusive equilibria.

The safer fallback is the orthogonality result: terminal-zero potential shaping preserves return differences and therefore cannot repair strategic-reporting incentives, whereas Q's non-potential transfers can. That result is sound but uses P's distinctive rank aggregation less directly and may be too generic by itself.
