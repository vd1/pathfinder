\section*{What, why, how}

\paragraph{What.} Pathfinder takes two corpora of papers, Q and P, and looks for pairs (q, p)
with something publishable between them: a result that neither paper states and that follows
from putting the two together. For every pair it keeps, it produces a record in increasing order
of readability and decreasing dependence on the process that made it: the \emph{thread} (a
ledger of everything the agents said to each other), the \emph{note} (what the agents agreed and
a judge accepted), the \emph{readable note} (the same, rewritten for a reader who never saw the
ledger), and, when the judge said the result would carry a paper, the \emph{paper} (written with
a literature search and reviewed).

\paragraph{Why a state machine.} The work is done by model calls that cost money, take minutes,
fail, and disagree. Writing the pipeline as a machine with explicit states, transitions and
budgets is what makes it re-implementable by a colleague on another corpus, auditable after the
fact (every decision traces to a file and a receipt), and safe to interrupt: a stop, a crash or an
exhausted budget leaves the machine in a named state from which it resumes, and a human can see
at a glance where each pair stands and what would move it.

\paragraph{How.} One research thread is one instance of the machine described below. Its subject
is a pair of papers together with the seed of an idea: in Pathfinder the seed is the scan's
connexion, rationale and scores, handed to the peers in their first prompt; in the agQSL instance
of the same machine the seed is a written idea artefact. The machine does not care where the seed
came from, only that the thread starts from a pair and a seed. The campaign is a set of these
instances plus a scheduler with a purse.
Three kinds of actor appear: \emph{agents}, model calls with a prompt and a bounded view of the
state, of which the two \emph{judges} (the verifier of a note, the reviewer of a paper) make the
only decisions; the \emph{pipeline}, code that builds, checks, records and moves the position;
and the \emph{owner}, whoever types in the terminal, a human or a delegated session, who raises
budgets, edits by hand and settles what is paused. The agents of one thread share one append-only
ledger; that ledger, the files beside it and a position record are the whole state of the
thread. Everything below is what the code does; the builder prompt points here for the shape to
re-implement.

\section{Global state of a thread}

\paragraph{Position.} \file{stage}, where the machine is; \file{status}, one of \file{running},
\file{stopped}, \file{BLOCKED} or a terminal word; the counters \file{round} (research rounds
used), \file{repairs} (note repairs used), the paper's \file{round} and the editor's
\file{attempt}.

\paragraph{Memory,} all files under \file{threads/<pair>/}:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item \file{inputs/}: the two papers, flattened \TeX{} or PDF text, and their metadata; the
    seed, the pair's row of \file{scan.jsonl}, is read from the campaign level.
  \item \file{ledger.jsonl}: append-only attributed entries of kind idea, finding, objection,
    correction, intention, ready or review; the peers' shared memory and the verifier's evidence.
  \item one directory per peer (\file{ada/}, \file{emmy/}, \ldots): that peer's derivations,
    scripts and checks. The peers are a campaign setting (\file{peers}); two by default, three
    were tested.
  \item \file{<pair>.tex}: the consolidated note; \file{<pair>.verdict.json}: every verdict with
    the digest of the note it judged.
  \item \file{paper/}: the paper, its bibliography, its search record, the pipeline's checks per
    round, and every review with the digest of the paper it judged.
  \item \file{edited/}: the readable note (\file{note.tex}, \file{references.bib},
    \file{note.pdf}).
  \item \file{status.json}, \file{paper/paper.json}, \file{edited/edit.json}: the position of
    each stage; \file{lock}: the pid of the runner working the thread.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Campaign level.} \file{fetch.json} (the two queries and how far each side has been
paged), \file{Q.jsonl} and \file{P.jsonl}, \file{scan.jsonl}, \file{shortlist.json},
\file{receipts.jsonl} (the only spend figure), and the markers \file{stop.json} and
\file{health.json}.

\section{The ledger}

The ledger is the one piece of state several agents write. Its rules are what make the peers a
group rather than a set of monologues:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item Entries are appended through a helper, never edited; each carries a sequence number, an
    actor, a kind and a time. Peers read the whole ledger before every call.
  \item \emph{ready} is a claim about the ledger as it stands: it is void as soon as anyone
    adds a substantive entry (anything but ready or review) after it. A peer whose ready still
    stands is not called again in that round; it waits for its partners.
  \item The round ends when every peer's ready stands, or when the round's allowance is out.
  \item The verifier writes its verdict into the ledger as a review entry, so the next round's
    peers, and the consolidator on a repair, read the judge's reasons where they read everything
    else.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Terminal words of the research thread: \st{draft}, \st{pause}, \st{pause-on-iterate},
\st{pause-on-revise}. Terminal words of the paper: \st{accepted}, \st{pause-on-amend}. Endings
of the editor: \st{done}, \st{blocked}. Every \st{pause-on-x} means ``the loop named X ran out of
its budget with the judge still asking''; the owner can raise the budget and resume from the
recorded stage, or, for the paper, edit it by hand and ask for one more review. The editor runs
once for every terminal thread, whatever its ending, so that even a paused pair leaves a readable
account.

\file{stopped} and \file{BLOCKED} are not endings. \file{stopped} is a drain checkpoint (a stop
marker, a transport failure, a kill) and resumes at the recorded stage on the next start.
\file{BLOCKED} is an unreadable decision or a missing output: the reply is saved beside the
position (for the verifier, \file{verify-unreadable.txt}), the runner never re-admits the pair,
and \file{reconcile} names the one safe action.

\section{Transitions}

Each transition is one or more model calls, or a mechanical step by the pipeline or the owner.
``Sees'' is the part of the global state the agent can read; ``writes'' is what it may change.
Nothing else is visible or writable, which is what makes the record auditable.

\medskip\noindent Between transitions the pipeline itself, with no model, does the checks that
gate the next state: it builds the \LaTeX, verifies that every citation exists and is used and
that arXiv identifiers carry their titles, refuses an edited note whose references do not check,
records digests, and writes the position. The verifier's and reviewer's judgements are the only
decisions; every other arrow is mechanical or the owner's.

\noindent \file{scan.jsonl} is append-only, so a longer corpus only adds rows; a threshold cut
re-selects over all rows and keeps every pair that has a thread. \file{explore} runs the loop
\file{fetch --more}, \file{scan}, \file{select --min-score} for a number of passes. The runner
then admits whatever is \st{new} on the shortlist, so research can run while the corpus grows.

\noindent Every model call appends a receipt, so the campaign budget is checked against the sum
of receipts, never against an estimate alone. A call that times out with no usage is charged at
\file{call\_estimate\_usd}. Owner rounds are not budgeted: the owner is the budget.

\section{Interrupts and hazards}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item A stop marker (operator, guard, or first Ctrl-C) stops admission; calls in flight land
    and write their checkpoints; the runner exits; the next start resumes each pair at its
    recorded stage.
  \item No session within the grace time is a transport failure: no receipt, the pair is
    \file{stopped}, two in a row set the health marker and pause admission until a probe
    succeeds. The \file{paper} and \file{edit} commands skip the failed pair and continue.
  \item \file{reconcile} reads the position of one pair and names the one safe action: start,
    resume peers, run consolidate, run verify, or nothing; \file{--apply} performs it.
  \item The lock protects a thread from two runners. It does not protect the paper or editor
    stage from two concurrent commands on the same pair; that is an operator error the code
    does not yet catch.
\end{itemize}
