# Call receipts: specification and implementation

17 September 2026, 07:26. Status: carried out the same day, after Codex's review
(see the end). All four parts are in: the transport and its tests, the
monitor, README.md and BUILDER.md, and the note with its rebuilt PDF.

## Why

The specification in `notes/pathfinder-engine.tex` is precise about control
and thin about records. It says calls "append receipts recording the usage
reported by the provider and the available cost estimate" and stops there.
Its own appendix then reports that the pilot could not be repriced, because
dollars were kept without the token counts behind them. A reimplementation
can follow every sentence of the note and end in the same place.

Checked in the code at commit `476f1b3`:

- A call that never opens a session writes no receipt (`transport.py:122`),
  and README.md and BUILDER.md both describe that as intended.
- A killed call with no usage is charged `call_estimate_usd` in the same
  `cost` field as a reported cost (`transport.py:131`), so an estimate reads
  as a measurement. Missing token counts are stored as 0.
- The two backends mean different things by `input_tokens`. A recorded Codex
  event reads `input_tokens 85160, cached_input_tokens 9088, output_tokens
  1156, reasoning_output_tokens 796`: the cached tokens sit inside the input
  total. Claude's `input_tokens` leaves cache reads out. The receipt stores
  both under one name, and drops Codex's `cache_write_input_tokens` and
  `reasoning_output_tokens` altogether.

## What changes

### 1. The note: one subsection, "Call receipts"

Placed after "Allowances". Provider-neutral, about a page. It requires:

- One receipt per call the transport attempts: launch failures, calls that
  never open a session and timeouts included. This is not a promise of
  durable accounting across an abrupt crash of the runner.
- What a receipt holds: time, thread, stage, actor, backend, requested model,
  elapsed seconds, outcome (completed, error, timeout, no session, launch
  failed), the
  provider's usage counters as the provider reported them, and the cost with
  its basis.
- Unknown stays unknown. A counter the provider did not report is null and
  never defaults to zero; a reported zero stays zero. Whatever counters did
  arrive are kept, also on a failed or killed call, where the outcome tells
  the reader they may be partial.
- Cost, in USD, is `reported` by the provider, or `priced` locally from
  reported counters, or null. A priced receipt carries the rates applied, so
  it stays readable after the price table changes. A priced amount is an
  approximation, not a reconstructed provider charge: today it ignores cached
  rates. Later repricing is analysis over receipts, not a receipt field.
- Counters are kept raw, with the backend named, because providers count
  differently. Any normalised breakdown is derived later from the raw
  counters and their documented meaning.
- The guarantee, stated as such: enough is recorded to reconstruct what a
  campaign consumed and, where the provider reports enough, to reprice it;
  what cannot be known is visibly unknown.
- The admission guard may count a call of unknown cost at the per-call
  estimate. That caution belongs to the guard and is not written into the
  receipt.

Two items join the conformance appendix: a call that fails to launch or
never opens a session leaves a receipt with null usage and null cost; a
killed call keeps whatever usage was reported, null when none was, and the
guard still counts it.

One sentence in the prefix-sharing paragraph is tightened: the reusable part
is the leading papers and the ledger entries already present; the note and
the instruction after them change between calls.

### 2. README.md and BUILDER.md

- Replace "no receipt" for a transport failure with "a receipt with no
  usage" (`README.md:151`, BUILDER.md "Transport").
- Correct the prefix sentence (`README.md:171`, `BUILDER.md:151`), which has
  the direction reversed and claims the whole head carries over.
- Extend the receipt description (`README.md:95`, `BUILDER.md:113`) to the
  fields above.

### 3. `pathfinder/transport.py`

- Write a receipt on the no-session path too, and when the executable
  cannot be launched, which today raises out of `call` with no receipt.
- Store `usage`: the provider's last reported counter object verbatim, or
  null when none arrived.
- Store `cost` and `cost_basis` (`reported`, `priced` with the `rates`
  applied, or null with null cost). Price only from reported counters.
  Remove the estimate from the receipt.
- Add `outcome` and `v: 2`. Receipts without `v` are the old format, where 0
  may mean unknown; readers treat them as they do today.
- Keep `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cache_read`, `cache_write` and
  `prefix_read` as they are, for the monitor, but null when not reported.
- `spend()` returns known cost plus `call_estimate_usd` for each receipt of
  unknown cost, so the guard stays conservative. Found while carrying this
  out: a call that never reached a session must not be charged, or the probes
  the runner makes during an outage would exhaust the budget on their own.
  The guard therefore charges what it charged before, killed calls, and the
  new failure receipts only show in the record.

`monitor.py` sums with `or 0`, which would show incomplete accounting as
total spend. Its figure becomes "known cost", and the state document gains
the number of calls of unknown cost beside it. The guard's estimate is not
shown as spend.

### 4. Tests

In `tests/test_transport.py`, with the existing fake CLI:

- no session, and a missing executable: a receipt exists, usage null, cost
  null, outcome set;
- killed after the session opens: outcome `timeout`, usage null when nothing
  was reported and kept when something was;
- a missing counter gives null and no zero-cost receipt, a reported zero stays
  zero, on either backend;
- Codex: the raw counters survive, reasoning and cache-write included;
- `spend()` counts an unknown-cost receipt at the estimate;
- over a mix of old and new receipts the monitor reports known cost and the
  count of unknown-cost calls, each meaning what it says.

## Left out, on purpose

- Attempt identifiers and retry links. Retry identity is not recorded and
  must not be inferred from adjacent receipts: an ordinary next call can carry
  the same thread, stage and actor. Nothing in the present accounting needs it.
- Start and end timestamps. The receipt has its end time and the seconds.
- Effort and the provider's actual model name. The transport does not set
  the first or reliably receive the second.
- Correcting the Codex pricing of cached input. It needs cached-token prices
  in every campaign's table. With raw counters and the applied rates kept, any
  receipt can be repriced later, and until then the amount is labelled an
  approximation.
  Revisited the next day, when `gpt-6-astra` joined the price tables: an entry
  may now give `cached_input_per_m`, and Codex's cached tokens are then priced
  apart. It is optional per model, so no other table had to change. In the
  recorded Astra experiments 90% of input was cached and flat pricing
  overstated the cost 4.5 times. The doubled tier above 272,000 input tokens
  is still left alone: a receipt sums a call's requests, so the tier cannot be
  read from it, and the Codex CLI's 258,000-token window stays below it.
- Rewriting old receipts. They stay as they are and are read as `v` 1.
- Provider adapters in the note. The note names the obligation; the adapter
  is the transport.

## Review

Codex (gpt-6-astra, "Spec feedback" pane) reviewed the draft on 17 September
2026. Its five required changes are all in, each in its smallest form: keep
partial usage; null only when nothing is reported or derivable, zero when
zero is reported; "known cost" and an unknown-cost count in the monitor; a
receipt for launch failures and a bounded guarantee; rates and currency so a
priced cost can be read later. It agreed that attempt identifiers, separate
timestamps, effort and model fields, and cache-aware pricing can wait, and
asked that retries not be inferred from adjacency, now stated above.

Not taken from the review: a separate flag for partial usage (the outcome
already says it), a currency field (the note fixes USD), and any machinery
for an immutable campaign configuration (the rates travel in the receipt).
